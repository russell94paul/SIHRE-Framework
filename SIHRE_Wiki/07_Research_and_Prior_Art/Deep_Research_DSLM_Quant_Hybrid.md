# DSLM Features for a SIHRE Quant Stack

## Bottom line

Yes: a domain-specific language model can emit structured outputs that are then consumed as features by a downstream tabular model such as LightGBM, and the strongest current evidence suggests that this is often a better design than asking the language model to trade directly. Across finance and adjacent tabular-learning research, the pattern that keeps reappearing is hybridisation: LLM-derived signals, embeddings, summaries, or rule-like outputs are fused with conventional numeric features and improve prediction when they are treated as typed inputs to a downstream learner rather than as an end-to-end decision-maker. This is visible in LLM-Boost, which uses LLM predictions as a starting point for gradient-boosted trees; in finance papers that combine prompt-extracted textual features with GBDT/XGBoost; in earnings-call frameworks that use LLM-extracted signals for volatility prediction; and in stock-movement studies that add sentiment outputs to conventional forecasting models. citeturn24view0turn5view1turn3view1turn29view4turn25view1

For your SIHRE design, the best-backed implementation is not "free-text chain-of-thought as a feature". It is a constrained schema that decomposes the DSLM's judgement into small, typed, auditable feature families: atomic condition flags, calibrated scalar scores, uncertainty diagnostics, and compressed semantic features. The literature also argues for caution: raw chain-of-thought is often not fully faithful to the model's true decision process, and financial LLM backtests are unusually vulnerable to temporal contamination and memorisation. So the hybrid can be powerful, but only if you treat the DSLM as a governed signal generator with dated weights, strict as-of generation, and prompt/model version control. citeturn24view3turn37view3turn32view0turn37view2turn32view1

## Encoding DSLM outputs for LightGBM

The evidence supports the core premise that LLM outputs can sit beside traditional quant inputs. In non-financial tabular work, contextual LLM embeddings added to ensemble classifiers such as XGBoost and CatBoost often improve performance, and LLM-Boost explicitly shows that fusing LLM outputs with GBDTs can outperform either component alone across a broad range of sample sizes. In finance, "Text Meets Numbers" finds that prompt-based LLM extraction plus financial indicators improves earnings forecasting across Gradient Boosting Decision Trees and XGBoost; ECC Analyzer uses LLM-derived text and audio features from earnings calls for volatility prediction; and stock-movement work finds that LLM sentiment features can improve several downstream time-series models. citeturn23search7turn24view0turn5view1turn3view1turn29view4

The most defensible encoding pattern for a DSLM that has been trained on ICT/Smart Money concepts is a layered schema:

- **Atomic setup-state features.** Emit one column per condition or motif, such as `liquidity_sweep_present`, `FVG_present`, `market_structure_shift_present`, `OTE_zone_hit`, `session_alignment`, and `HTF_bias_agrees`. Also emit counts and ratios such as `entry_conditions_met = 4`, `entry_conditions_total = 6`, and `entry_conditions_frac = 0.667`. Low-dimensional, interpretable features like these are precisely what recent work on LLM-based feature generation argues for when the downstream learner is tabular and interpretability matters. citeturn24view3turn24view2

- **Calibrated scalar judgement features.** Emit bounded numeric scores such as `dslm_direction_score`, `setup_quality`, `displacement_zscore`, `liquidity_imbalance_score`, `semantic_pattern_score`, and `verbal_confidence_raw`. Then calibrate these scores ex post on the training window so that, for example, `0.72` means something like "historically maps to a 58% conditional hit rate under this prompt/model/version". Adjacent LLM calibration work shows that generated confidence is often usable only after explicit calibration, and finance studies already compare confidence/probability-score representations against discrete labels in downstream prediction. citeturn8view2turn8view3turn29view4

- **Uncertainty and consistency features.** A single DSLM score is usually weaker than a small distribution of DSLM responses. In practice, useful columns are `self_consistency_vote_share`, `semantic_entropy`, `kernel_language_entropy`, `verifier_score`, `stepwise_confidence_robustness`, and `reasoning_length`. These are not yet standard finance features, but they are supported by the broader LLM uncertainty literature and are directly relevant if your DSLM is supposed to emit "confidence". citeturn8view1turn8view0turn6search0turn10view0turn10view2

- **Compressed semantic features.** If you want the model to capture nuance beyond a small fixed schema, do not feed raw rationale text to LightGBM. Instead, generate a finance-tuned sentence embedding for the rationale or matched motif, reduce it on the training set only, and pass a small number of components or similarity scores downstream. Financial studies already use embeddings, PCA, semantic similarity, and aligned multi-encoder spaces to turn text into predictive numeric inputs. citeturn25view1turn5view2turn8view5turn5view3turn29view0

A practical implication follows from the chain-of-thought literature: do not equate a fluent rationale with a faithful one. CoT traces can be plausible but unfaithful, and both causal-faithfulness and logical-verification work show that reasoning traces should be treated as noisy observables. In a production quant stack, the safer move is to use **derived metrics of reasoning quality** as features, while keeping the full rationale for audit and debugging rather than for direct ingestion by the booster. citeturn37view3turn10view1turn9search0

## State of the art in finance

The state of the art has moved from plain sentiment labels towards richer, multimodal and hybrid pipelines. Financial foundation models such as BloombergGPT, FinGPT, FinTral, and Open-FinLLMs all reflect the same trajectory: more finance-specific corpora, more structured and multimodal inputs, and more emphasis on downstream financial tasks rather than generic NLP only. Surveys of LLMs in finance and quantitative investment describe two dominant roles today: LLMs as predictors that extract or reason over information, and LLMs as agents that orchestrate broader workflows. They also note that practical deployment remains early and that hybrid integration with classical quant models is a major live research direction rather than a solved practice. citeturn22search0turn22search10turn22search5turn5view4turn1view3turn1view6turn1view7

Within systematic trading specifically, the cutting edge is not "LLM alone". It is multimodal fusion, retrieval, and ensemble reuse of pretrained representations. The FinRL contest has explicitly focused on combining LLM-generated signals with reinforcement learning for trading. Open-FinLLMs reports strong performance across text, tables, time series, charts, and trading tasks. FinSrag introduces a financial time-series retriever that improves an LLM forecaster by retrieving informative historical segments rather than relying on surface-level similarity. FinAnchor aligns frozen representations from multiple encoders in a common space for financial prediction. Together, these papers point to the same architectural conclusion for SIHRE: the frontier is heterogeneous signal composition, not monolithic end-to-end prompting. citeturn1view4turn5view4turn25view2turn5view3

From a feature-engineering perspective, the useful finance-specific pattern is increasingly clear. The strongest published gains usually come from one of four approaches: extracting richer sentiment and topic signals than keyword methods, augmenting conventional indicators with embeddings or summaries, retrieving historical analogues to the current regime, or aligning several pretrained encoders and letting a smaller downstream model learn the final decision boundary. That is exactly the design space your DSLM-plus-LightGBM hybrid inhabits. citeturn5view1turn5view2turn25view1turn25view2turn5view3

## Confidence, coherence and similarity signals

There is not yet a large, mature finance literature that uses **chain coherence** or **confidence calibration** as first-class trading features in the same way volatility or momentum are used. But there is enough adjacent evidence to justify experimenting with them. Finance papers already use confidence-style outputs in tangible ways: stock-prediction work compares discrete sentiment labels with probability/confidence representations from transformer models; FinSrag uses generation logits as a proxy for the relevance of retrieved historical sequences; and recent disclosure research shows that LLM extraction combined with semantic similarity yields stronger return predictability than an NER baseline. These are not identical to your proposed "reasoning confidence" feature, but they are very close in spirit. citeturn29view4turn25view2turn29view0

The general LLM literature provides several good candidates for financial feature engineering. Self-consistency gives you a vote share across multiple reasoning traces rather than a single answer. Self-verification yields an interpretable validation score for candidate answers. Semantic entropy and kernel language entropy quantify uncertainty over meaning rather than wording, which is much closer to the relevant notion of uncertainty in market reasoning tasks. APRICOT and related calibration work show that confidence can be predicted and improved even when the interface is mostly textual. Stepwise confidence work adds a trajectory-level notion of robustness across the reasoning path. citeturn8view1turn10view0turn8view0turn6search0turn8view2turn10view2

For a quant implementation, the most promising features are therefore not "the full chain". They are things like `consensus_across_samples`, `semantic_entropy`, `verifier_acceptance`, `faithfulness_proxy`, and `similarity_to_profitable_archetypes`. The supporting evidence for the last item is especially interesting: financial semantic-similarity work and "From Text to Alpha" suggest that the way disclosures change relative to earlier economically meaningful patterns contains return-relevant information. In your SIHRE setting, a DSLM that scores similarity to dated, historically profitable ICT/SMC setups could plausibly be valuable, provided the pattern library itself is strictly point-in-time and the similarity model is also dated or evaluated out of sample. citeturn8view5turn29view0turn28search7

## Anti-lookahead and evaluation governance

This is the make-or-break issue. The strongest recent papers are unusually clear: pretrained LLMs can leak future information into historical financial analyses, and prompting alone does not fix the problem. Sarkar and Vafa show direct evidence of temporal lookahead bias and explicitly state that the remedy is to use models whose pretraining data contains only language produced prior to the analysis period. The "Memorization Problem" paper goes further, showing that LLMs memorise economic and financial data during covered periods, that instructions to respect historical boundaries fail, and that memorisation extends to embeddings. DatedGPT and Chronologically Consistent LLMs both arise as responses to this exact problem. citeturn32view0turn1view2turn37view2turn32view1

The design consequence is stark. If your DSLM was trained on corpora that include post-event commentary, realised outcomes, hindsight explanations, or future-labelled examples from the very periods you backtest, then any impressive in-sample result is fundamentally ambiguous: it may reflect reasoning, memorisation, or both. The literature argues that you cannot identify genuine forecasting skill inside the model's knowledge window merely by telling the model to "ignore the future". citeturn1view2turn37view2turn32view0

The strongest control stack for a production SIHRE platform is therefore:

- **Use dated model weights whenever possible.** DatedGPT trains separate models with strict annual cut-offs, and ChronoBERT/ChronoGPT do the same at larger system level. citeturn37view2turn32view1  
- **Generate DSLM features in a true walk-forward loop.** For each evaluation date, freeze model weights, prompt version, retrieval index, and schema definitions as of that date. citeturn37view2turn32view1turn26view0  
- **Restrict retrieval and corpora to as-of sources only.** Time-aware pretraining and time-consistent retrieval are both now active research areas precisely because ex-post textual context contaminates apparent skill. citeturn37view2turn25view2  
- **Neutralise entity leakage when backtesting text-only tasks.** Glasserman and Lin find that anonymising company identifiers can outperform original headlines in-sample because general company knowledge can distract the model and distort sentiment measurement. citeturn37view0  
- **Use finance-specific leakage controls in downstream validation.** Current financial ML work continues to support purging, embargoing, and CPCV-style out-of-sample testing as defences against overlap leakage and backtest overfitting. citeturn34search0turn34search12  
- **Run contamination diagnostics.** The Lookahead Propensity test is a concrete new method for estimating whether prompt content appeared in pretraining and whether forecast accuracy is suspiciously correlated with that likelihood. citeturn37view1  

On prompt-versioned A/B testing, direct research in systematic trading is still thin, but the methodological pieces are there. Regression-testing work shows that model updates can flip the best prompt choice and argues that both prompt versions and model versions must be tracked. Empirical work on prompt evolution shows that prompt changes are often poorly documented and can silently change behaviour. Prompt-optimisation research, including DSPy, GRIPS, adversarial in-context learning, and bandit-based prompt-strategy selection, provides offline mechanisms for prompt search rather than ad hoc trial-and-error. In finance specifically, Zhuokai Chen reports that prompt variations changed the efficacy of an LLM trading filter, while identical prompts yielded consistent outputs. citeturn26view0turn26view1turn26view2turn26view3turn27search0turn27search2turn27search3turn30view0

For SIHRE, that means prompt A/B testing should be treated as **model governance**, not prompt tinkering. The right unit of comparison is not "which prompt sounds better", but "which prompt version generates features that most improve downstream out-of-sample information coefficient, calibration, turnover-adjusted PnL, and stability across purged date blocks". That recommendation is an inference from the governance and prompt-optimisation literature, but it is the one most consistent with the evidence reviewed above. citeturn26view0turn26view2turn26view3turn27search0

## How to make the hybrid stronger

The most promising next step is a **residual hybrid**: let the DSLM produce a prior score or prior log-odds for the trade thesis, then let LightGBM learn the residual conditional on conventional quant inputs such as realised volatility, spread, order-book imbalance, session effects, and execution context. That architecture is directly analogous to LLM-Boost, where tree models improve on transformer priors by learning what the pretrained model misses. In a trading context, it gives you a clean decomposition: the DSLM estimates narrative/setup quality, while the booster adjusts for market mechanics and regime-specific failure modes. citeturn24view0

A second strong direction is **uncertainty-gated routing**. Instead of always trusting the DSLM, route the observation between a quant-only branch, a DSLM-enhanced branch, and a no-trade branch based on semantic entropy, self-consistency, self-verification, or stepwise confidence robustness. This is an inference from the uncertainty literature rather than an established fund standard, but it is well supported by the fact that modern LLM uncertainty measures outperform naive token confidence and that self-verification scores can be meaningful. citeturn8view0turn6search0turn10view0turn10view2

A third direction is **retrieval-to-feature memory**, where the DSLM does not merely "reason from scratch" about the current setup. Instead, it retrieves dated historical analogues and emits features such as `similarity_to_profitable_pattern`, `similarity_to_failed_pattern`, `top_k_outcome_dispersion`, or `retrieved_pattern_consensus`. FinSrag shows that better historical retrieval can improve financial forecasting beyond surface pattern matching, while disclosure work using semantic similarity shows that semantically grounded comparisons can be return-predictive. citeturn25view2turn29view0

A fourth direction is **multi-view signal generation** rather than single-view prompting. The same market state can be described as an ICT setup, a market-microstructure state, a macro-event state, or a cross-asset regime. Open-FinLLMs, FinAnchor, and multimodal stock-prediction papers all suggest that gains often come from combining multiple representational views rather than pushing one model harder. In SIHRE terms, that argues for several DSLM sub-prompts or sub-models that each specialise in a different ontology, with the downstream booster learning when they are complementary. citeturn5view4turn5view3turn25view4turn5view5

A fifth direction is **time-partitioned DSLMs**. If temporal leakage is a real risk and market structure changes over time, then a family of regime- or date-specific DSLMs may be better than one universal model. DatedGPT and ChronoBERT/ChronoGPT show the feasibility of building temporally bounded language models, and Two Sigma's public comments on point-in-time models point in the same direction. For a live SIHRE system, a regime-aware selector over dated DSLM variants could be more robust than a single timeless model. citeturn37view2turn32view1turn18search0

## Open questions and limitations

A few important gaps remain. First, the direct literature on using **generative reasoning metrics themselves** as alpha features is still small; most of the strongest evidence comes from adjacent uncertainty, calibration, and semantic-similarity research rather than from mature systematic-trading deployments. Second, many finance papers in this area are still preprints, are tested on limited universes or short windows, and often understate costs, latency, and contamination risk. Third, public disclosures from major quant funds are sparse by design, so any claim beyond what is explicitly stated in official material quickly becomes speculative. citeturn1view3turn1view7turn12search7turn28search16turn35search0turn19search0turn21news41

The highest-confidence conclusion, therefore, is not that DSLMs will replace quant models. It is that a **well-governed DSLM can be a high-value structured signal generator** inside a broader quant stack, especially when its outputs are typed, calibrated, uncertainty-aware, semantically grounded, and evaluated under strict point-in-time controls. That is the architecture most aligned with the evidence today, and it is also the one least likely to fail for the wrong reasons. citeturn24view0turn5view1turn29view0turn32view0turn37view2turn26view0
