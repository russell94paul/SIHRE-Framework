# NeuroFusion-13 and the state of heterogeneous reasoning architectures

## Overall assessment

Your proposed **Self-Improving Heterogeneous Reasoning Ensemble** is best understood as a **new architecture label for a real but still loosely-defined design space**, rather than as a term already standardised in academic literature. In the literature I found, the exact phrase **"heterogeneous reasoning ensemble"** does not appear to be an established term of art; instead, adjacent work clusters around **heterogeneous reasoning**, **compound AI systems**, **neuro-symbolic AI**, **GraphRAG / KG-guided reasoning**, **heterogeneous LLM ensembles**, and **tool-orchestrated reasoning systems**. Older work used "heterogeneous reasoning" for systems that combine different representational forms or knowledge sources, while recent work uses phrases such as **"adaptive skill-based routing for heterogeneous reasoning"** and **"ensemble learning for heterogeneous large language models"**. citeturn20view0turn21search1turn21search16turn22search8turn9search1turn9search0

In public finance and trading research, there are already systems that look **partly** like what you are designing: **TradExpert** uses multiple specialised LLM experts over news, market, alpha-factor, and fundamentals data; **TradingGPT** uses multi-agent trading with layered memory and debate; **LLMoE** uses an LLM as the router inside a trading MoE; **TRACE** combines temporal knowledge graphs, symbolic rule mining, LLM-guided graph exploration, and interpretable stock prediction; **FinDKG** builds dynamic financial knowledge graphs from financial news; and **FinErva** focuses on interpretable multimodal financial reasoning. But none of these, on the public record, matches your exact four-signal architecture **plus** persistent self-improving graph memory **plus** downstream quantitative execution models **plus** reasoning-level fusion across genuinely different modalities. citeturn28search0turn28academia32turn28academia31turn26view0turn29search2turn19view0

So the short verdict is: **yes, there is a recognisable family resemblance**; **no, your exact framing is not already a canonical category**. If presented academically, NeuroFusion-13 would be strongest as a **finance-specific compound AI / neuro-symbolic reasoning system with typed intermediate-state fusion and persistent graph memory**, rather than as a reinvention of classical ensemble learning. citeturn30view0turn11search3turn11search4turn11search10

## Whether something like this already exists

In conventional machine learning, **heterogeneous ensembles** absolutely exist, including in financial prediction; however, they almost always mean **combining different base learners or different data views at the prediction stage**. The forecast-combination literature is long-established, and financial/stock prediction papers already combine heterogeneous models and data sources because different models tend to perform well in different periods or regimes. But that literature usually fuses **forecasts, rankings, or probabilities**, not **reasoning traces, symbolic evidence, retrieval chains, and persistent memory states**. citeturn17search11turn17search8turn17search14turn3search5

The closest finance-native analogue to your idea is probably **TradExpert**, because it already splits financial reasoning across specialised experts for distinct information types and then synthesises them with a general expert. That said, TradExpert is still fundamentally a **Mixture of Expert LLMs**. Its experts are heterogeneous mainly in **input specialisation**, not in the deeper sense you mean by different **kinds of intelligence** such as retrieval, symbolic graph memory, gradient-boosted quant models, causal modules, or self-improving evaluation memory. citeturn28search0turn28search10

The recent finance paper **TRACE** gets closer to your stated philosophy than classic ensembles do. TRACE explicitly claims a **unified methodology** that combines **symbolic priors**, **dynamic graph exploration**, and **LLM-guided decision making** inside a temporal financial knowledge graph, and it also reports that adding an XGBoost baseline yields only a modest marginal gain. That makes TRACE highly relevant to NeuroFusion-13, because it shows that the literature is already moving from "better prediction" to **interpretable, path-based reasoning over heterogeneous evidence**. Even so, TRACE still does not appear to include your proposed combination of vector retrieval, a domain-specific language model that emits structured downstream features, and a persistent trade/backtest/evaluation memory graph. citeturn26view0

Outside finance, the exact terminology is closer still. **DeePEn** studies **heterogeneous LLM ensembles** at the distribution level; **Mixture of Thoughts** adds latent-space collaboration among heterogeneous experts; **Dynamic Ensemble Reasoning** learns sequential expert-routing paths; **LLM-Blender** ranks and fuses candidate outputs; **Atlas** jointly orchestrates heterogeneous model-tool combinations through routing and reinforcement learning; and **Symbolic Mixture-of-Experts** explicitly frames its goal as skill-based routing for **heterogeneous reasoning**. These are not your system, but they are the closest modern precedent for "reasoning-level fusion across heterogeneous components". citeturn9search1turn9search0turn23search0turn23search3turn24view0turn22search8

## Closest architectures and papers

The broadest umbrella for your proposal is **Compound AI Systems**. Berkeley's 2024 framing is especially relevant because it defines compound systems as AI systems built from multiple interacting components—multiple model calls, retrievers, and external tools—and argues that system design can outperform simple model scaling on reliability, control, freshness, and cost/performance trade-offs. That is almost exactly the conceptual move you are making: not asking one model to do everything, but engineering a higher-level reasoning system from diverse components. Later work on **system-level alignment** and **resource-efficient compound AI** pushes this further by treating the full system as the optimisation object, not the individual model. citeturn30view0turn30view2turn31view0

For your **vector DB retrieval plus knowledge graph** components, the closest academic line is the progression from **RAG** to **GraphRAG** and **KG-guided RAG**. Lewis et al. formalised RAG as combining parametric memory with an explicit non-parametric memory index. **GraphRAG** extended this idea to graph-based indexing and query-focused summarisation over complex corpora, while **KG2RAG** adds knowledge-graph-guided expansion and chunk organisation after semantic retrieval. **StructGPT** is also highly relevant because it separates **reading** from **reasoning** and iteratively gathers structured evidence through tool-like interfaces before reasoning over it. NeuroFusion-13's first and fourth signals map naturally onto this family. citeturn29search0turn13search0turn13search10turn29search1

For the **reasoning-over-structure** part of your design, the strongest conceptual neighbours are in **neuro-symbolic AI**. Recent surveys describe neuro-symbolic AI as fusing neural, symbolic, and often probabilistic methods to gain interpretability, robustness, and stronger reasoning, and there is now a specific survey on **neuro-symbolic reasoning over knowledge graphs**. Your persistent, regime-aware trade memory graph begins to look novel when it is not just a store of facts, but a **symbolic substrate for reasoning, retrieval, path scoring, and post-trade learning**. citeturn11search3turn11search4turn11search10

For your **domain-specific language model** that emits structured classifications which then feed downstream quant models, the nearest precedent is not trading per se, but **LLM-as-feature-engineer** work. **FeatLLM** shows that LLMs can generate features for tabular downstream predictors, and recent work on structured outputs shows that output schemas matter materially for downstream reliability. In finance specifically, **FinBERT** and **FinGPT** show that domain-specialised language models are already established, so a finance DSLM is not unprecedented; what is more unusual is using that model not as the final decision-maker, but as a **typed feature emitter** inside a broader quant stack. citeturn29search3turn8search19turn7search0turn7search1turn7search6

Within finance itself, the nearest public papers to your design are a small but important cluster. **Knowledge Graph-based Event Embedding Framework for Financial Quantitative Investments** already uses KG-derived event embeddings for quant investing. **FinDKG** uses LLMs to generate dynamic financial knowledge graphs and then analyses them with a graph model for thematic investing. **Knowledge Graph Construction for Stock Markets with LLM-Based Explainable Reasoning** explicitly positions itself as multi-hop, explainable financial reasoning over a stock-market KG. **FinErva** emphasises multimodal, interpretable financial reasoning and chain-of-thought. Taken together, these papers show that your ingredients are individually credible; the gap is the **single architecture that fuses them all at reasoning level**. citeturn14search0turn29search2turn27view0turn19view0

## Fusion mechanisms for the four core signals

The most practical way to fuse your four core signals is to avoid jumping directly to a final trade score and instead create a **typed intermediate reasoning state**. In that state, vector retrieval does not output "bullish" or "bearish"; it outputs a ranked set of evidence objects. The DSLM does not output prose; it outputs a fixed schema such as `{setup_type, market_structure_state, liquidity_event, conditions_met, conditions_unmet, confidence, invalidation}`. Quant models output calibrated probabilistic forecasts or residual signals. The knowledge graph outputs temporally valid paths, motifs, neighbour regimes, and prior trade analogues. This approach is much closer to **StructGPT's reading-then-reasoning**, **GraphRAG/KG2RAG's evidence expansion**, and **FeatLLM's feature-generation** than to classic late fusion. citeturn29search1turn13search0turn13search10turn29search3

For the **evidence aggregation layer**, **Reciprocal Rank Fusion** is attractive precisely because your signal families live on incomparable score scales. Retrieval cosine scores, KG path scores, DSLM confidences, and gradient-boosted margins should not be naively normalised and added. RRF was designed to combine rankings without relying on score calibration, and modern hybrid retrieval work continues to use it because dense and sparse systems are complementary but poorly score-aligned. In NeuroFusion-13, RRF is most natural for fusing **candidate evidence items**—documents, graph paths, analogue trades, and rule hits—before any higher-order reasoning occurs. citeturn5search1turn6search0turn6search18

For the **reasoning layer**, the closest off-the-shelf pattern is a **router-plus-cross-attention** design. The router should be **regime-aware** and choose which modalities deserve compute on a given market state; for example, in a news-shock regime it may weight KG/event paths and DSLM classifications more heavily, while in quiet mean-reversion regimes it may emphasise boosted-tree signals and memory analogues. Once selected, modality-specific encoders can produce tokens or slots for a shared fusion block where cross-attention operates over **retrieval evidence, structured DSLM features, quant factors, and graph paths**. That design borrows from multimodal fusion surveys and from recent heterogeneous-model systems such as **Mixture of Thoughts**, **Dynamic Ensemble Reasoning**, and **Atlas**, all of which show that static all-expert invocation is often inferior to adaptive routing and interaction. citeturn12search1turn12search2turn12search9turn9search0turn23search0turn24view0

For the **symbolic governance layer**, a neuro-symbolic pattern is stronger than pure attention. TRACE is especially instructive here: it mines symbolic relational motifs, constrains the admissible reasoning paths, and then allows graph exploration and LLM-guided selection inside those constraints. A similar design would let NeuroFusion-13 use the knowledge graph not just as memory, but as a **constraint system**: for example, "do not long if the retrieved analogue cluster contains high-confidence failure under the current volatility regime", or "promotion of a long setup requires either confirmation from market-structure retrieval or a matching historical subgraph pattern". This is the point where your architecture most plausibly becomes a **reasoning system** rather than a dressed-up ensemble. citeturn26view0turn11search10turn29search1

The **self-improving** component should be treated as a fifth layer rather than an afterthought. The persistent knowledge graph can log not just trades, but the full reasoning trace: what was retrieved, what the DSLM classified, what the quant model forecast, which graph paths were activated, which regime the router inferred, and what the realised outcome was. That gives you a substrate for post-trade critique, route scoring, and counterfactual replay. This idea is adjacent to **Reflexion**, **Self-Refine**, and **STaR** on the language side, and to system-level compound-AI optimisation on the orchestration side; the novelty in finance would come from binding those ideas to a durable trade-memory graph rather than an ephemeral chat history. citeturn15search1turn15search2turn15search0turn30view2

## What would make NeuroFusion-13 genuinely novel

The four ingredients you named are **not individually novel**. Vector retrieval over a domain corpus is standard RAG; domain-specific finance language models already exist; downstream use of LLM-generated or LLM-extracted features is an active line of work; knowledge graphs and event embeddings are already used for finance; and trading systems built from multiple expert LLMs have already been published. If NeuroFusion-13 merely places these pieces side by side and averages their outputs, reviewers will likely see it as an incremental **compound system for trading**, not as a new architecture class. citeturn29search0turn7search0turn7search1turn29search3turn14search0turn28search0

What would make it more than incremental is the combination of **ontological heterogeneity**, **typed reasoning-state fusion**, **persistent self-improving memory**, and **system-level optimisation**. Ontological heterogeneity means the system is not merely using thirteen differently prompted LLMs; it is combining components with different epistemic roles: retrieval, structured semantic classification, statistical forecasting, symbolic graph reasoning, regime memory, adversarial challenge, and perhaps causal or evolutionary search. Typed reasoning-state fusion means those components meet in a shared machine-readable workspace before a decision is made. Persistent self-improving memory means the graph compounds from trades, backtests, and evaluations and changes routing or evidence selection over time. System-level optimisation means the full DAG is optimised and evaluated as a system, which is exactly the problem modern compound-AI work says matters. citeturn30view0turn30view2turn24view0turn26view0

A good academic novelty test is whether you can honestly claim all of the following. First, the system fuses **reasoning objects** rather than just final predictions or votes. Secondly, the graph memory is not passive storage but an active **temporal reasoning substrate**. Thirdly, the router is conditioned on **regime and uncertainty**, not merely query text. Fourthly, the system learns from realised outcomes at the **system level**, not solely through isolated model retraining. Fifthly, you can show that the combined system outperforms every strong ablation, including "best single signal", "late-fusion ensemble", "LLM-only MoE", and "KG+XGBoost only" baselines. Without those properties, "13 modalities" is mostly branding; with them, NeuroFusion-13 becomes a credible new finance architecture proposal. citeturn17search14turn17search20turn28search0turn26view0turn30view2

If you do write this up, the strongest positioning statement would be something like: **a regime-aware compound neuro-symbolic trading system that performs typed reasoning-state fusion across retrieval, language, graph, and statistical modules, with persistent post-trade memory and system-level self-improvement**. That framing is novel enough to be defensible, while still rooted in the actual literature instead of sounding disconnected from it. citeturn30view0turn11search3turn11search4turn24view0

## Limits, failure modes, and open questions

The main theoretical limit is that **more modalities do not automatically produce more intelligence**. Ensemble theory and forecast-combination research consistently find that gains come from **useful diversity** and error decorrelation, not from raw model count. If the extra modalities collapse onto the same latent view of the market, then adding the thirteenth signal often adds complexity, latency, and overfitting risk faster than it adds information. In that sense, the ceiling on NeuroFusion-13 is set less by "13" and more by whether the modalities truly disagree for good reasons. citeturn17search5turn17search20turn17search11turn17search14

The main practical limit in trading is **non-stationarity**. Financial markets exhibit regime change and concept drift, so a self-improving architecture can easily become a **self-reinforcing overfitter** if it treats past successful traces as timeless. The concept-drift literature in financial time series is clear that detection, adaptation, and evaluation under drift are first-class design problems, not operational details. A persistent knowledge graph helps only if it remembers **when** a pattern worked, **under what regime**, and **how quickly it decayed**. citeturn16search3turn16search4turn16search7turn16search34

A second practical limit is **calibration and comparability**. Your core signals are not naturally commensurate: vector retrieval outputs retrieval relevance, the DSLM outputs schema-level confidence, boosted trees output statistical class scores, and the graph emits path or motif strength. Rank-based fusion can help at the evidence stage, but once you move into decision fusion you still need careful calibration, abstention logic, and uncertainty accounting, otherwise the system will simply look sophisticated while hiding brittle score arithmetic underneath. citeturn5search1turn30view2

A third limit is **system-level optimisation difficulty**. Recent compound-AI work explicitly notes that end-to-end training is hard because component interactions are often non-differentiable and because system-level preferences do not trivially decompose into local preferences. That matters directly for NeuroFusion-13: improving the DSLM marginally may worsen the router; enriching the graph may slow the retriever; adding symbolic checks may improve precision but hurt recall; and optimising one component in isolation may degrade the whole DAG. In other words, the architecture's hardest problem is not "build thirteen experts", but "optimise the whole workflow without losing reliability". citeturn30view2turn31view1

The final limit is operational. Compound systems are measurably harder to run, monitor, and debug than single models. Berkeley's compound-AI analysis highlights the need for new tracing, data-ops, and monitoring practices, while recent systems work shows that better orchestration can materially improve runtime and energy efficiency. For a live trading stack, this means your architecture is only as credible as its **latency budget, fallback logic, trace observability, ablations, and live out-of-sample evaluation protocol**. Behaviour-driven evaluation is likely a better fit than static benchmarks, because the real question is how the system behaves under regime shifts, missing data, contradictory evidence, and post-trade regret. citeturn30view0turn31view0turn18search0

The biggest open questions are straightforward but non-trivial. Can you define a **canonical typed reasoning state** that all modalities can write to without excessive information loss? Can you prove that graph memory improves live decisions rather than merely preserving rationalisations? Can the router learn regime-conditioned modality selection without becoming another overfit model? And, most importantly, does reasoning-level fusion deliver better **risk-adjusted out-of-sample performance** than a brutally simple late-fusion benchmark built from the same ingredients? Those questions are unanswered in the public literature I found, and they are exactly where a NeuroFusion-13 paper could make a real contribution.
